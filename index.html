Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act
HIPAA Security Rule
45 CFR Part 164, Subpart C — Technical, Physical, and Administrative Safeguards for Protected Health Information (PHI)

The HIPAA Security Rule requires covered entities — medical practices, dental offices, therapists, mental health providers, and their business associates — to implement administrative, physical, and technical safeguards protecting electronic PHI (ePHI). Violations carry civil penalties from $100 to $50,000 per violation with annual caps up to $1.9 million per category.

Who it applies to
All healthcare providers handling ePHI
Enforced by
HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR)
Penalty range
$100 – $50,000 per violation
Key risk
Terminated employees retaining access
What HIPAA requires (technical safeguards)
§164
Access controls (§164.312(a)(1))
Unique user IDs, automatic logoff, encryption and decryption procedures for ePHI systems.
AUD
Audit controls (§164.312(b))
Hardware, software, and procedural mechanisms to record and examine activity in systems containing ePHI.
INT
Integrity (§164.312(c)(1))
Policies and procedures to protect ePHI from improper alteration or destruction.
TRS
Transmission security (§164.312(e)(1))
Technical measures to guard against unauthorized access when ePHI is transmitted over electronic networks.
RA
Risk analysis (Required)
Accurate and thorough assessment of potential risks and vulnerabilities to ePHI confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

Access control review & hardening

Continuous monitoring of ePHI systems

Payment Card Industry Data Security Standard
PCI-DSS v4.0
PCI Security Standards Council — Version 4.0, Fully Mandatory Since March 31, 2025

Any business that accepts, processes, stores, or transmits payment card data must comply with PCI-DSS. Version 4.0 became the only accepted version as of March 31, 2025. It introduces stronger authentication requirements, continuous monitoring mandates, and targeted risk analysis for each control. POS systems are one of the most commonly compromised attack surfaces in retail and hospitality.

Who it applies to
Any business taking card payments
Enforced by
Payment brands, acquiring banks
v4.0 mandatory since
March 31, 2025
High-risk system
POS terminals, card readers, Wi-Fi
Key PCI-DSS v4.0 requirements
R1
Network security controls (Req. 1)
Install and maintain network security controls — firewalls protecting cardholder data environments from untrusted networks.
R2
Secure configurations (Req. 2)
Apply secure configurations to all system components. Eliminate vendor defaults and unnecessary services.
R6
Vulnerability management (Req. 6)
Protect systems and networks from malicious software. Develop and maintain secure systems and software with timely patching.
R8
Strong authentication (Req. 8)
Multi-factor authentication now required for all access to the cardholder data environment. Phishing-resistant MFA preferred.
R10
Log all access (Req. 10)
Log and monitor all access to system components and cardholder data — automated log reviews now required daily.
R11
Regular testing (Req. 11)
Test security of systems and networks regularly — including vulnerability scanning and targeted risk analysis per control.
How Lotus covers your POS environment
POS & network segmentation review
We assess your POS terminals, card readers, and network layout — identifying whether your cardholder data environment is properly isolated from other systems.
Firewall & secure configuration hardening
Firewall rules reviewed and tightened, default credentials removed, unnecessary services disabled — addressing Requirements 1 and 2 directly.
MFA setup & patch management
Multi-factor authentication configured for all access to payment systems. Patch management oversight ensures timely updates — Requirement 6 and 8 coverage.
Continuous log monitoring
24/7 monitoring with daily automated log review alerts — meeting PCI-DSS v4.0's new automated monitoring requirement under Requirement 10.
PCI-DSS v4.0 is fully mandatory. If you take card payments, this applies to you.

Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act
FERPA
20 U.S.C. § 1232g; 34 CFR Part 99 — Protection of Student Education Records

FERPA protects the privacy of student education records. It applies to schools, tutoring centers, after-school programs, and any organization receiving federal funding that maintains student records. While FERPA is primarily a data access and disclosure law, its security obligations have grown increasingly technical — and breaches of student data trigger mandatory notification requirements.

Who it applies to
Schools, tutoring centers, ed-tech platforms
Enforced by
U.S. Department of Education (FPCO)
Key risk
Unauthorized access to student information systems
Paired with
NIST CSF, COPPA (under-13 data)
FERPA security obligations
ACC
Access limitation
Only authorized school officials with a legitimate educational interest may access student records. Role-based access controls required.
3RD
Third-party data handling
Vendors with access to student data must operate under a school official designation or data sharing agreement with appropriate protections.
BRE
Breach notification
Pennsylvania law (Act 33) requires notification of students and families when education records are breached. Documentation is essential.
SEC
Reasonable security measures
FERPA does not specify technical controls but courts and regulators expect reasonable technical safeguards around student data systems.
How Lotus protects student data environments
Student information system (SIS) access audit
We review who has access to your SIS, LMS, and parent portal — identifying over-permissioned accounts, former staff with active access, and unapproved third-party integrations.
Role-based access control implementation
Configuring access controls so teachers, administrators, and support staff only see the student records relevant to their role — reducing insider risk and accidental exposure.
Network monitoring for student data systems
Continuous monitoring of the systems hosting student records, with alerts on anomalous access patterns that could indicate unauthorized use or a breach in progress.
Breach documentation support
In the event of a breach, Lotus documents the scope, affected records, and timeline — the evidence trail required for Pennsylvania's mandatory notification process.
Serving private schools, tutoring centers, and after-school programs across Chester and Delaware Counties.

Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act — Safeguards Rule
GLBA Safeguards Rule
16 CFR Part 314 — FTC Standards for Safeguarding Customer Information. Updated Rule Effective June 9, 2023.

The FTC's updated GLBA Safeguards Rule significantly expanded the technical requirements for financial institutions — a category that includes CPAs, tax preparers, financial advisors, mortgage brokers, auto dealers, and any business providing financial products or services. The 2023 update introduced specific technical controls previously absent from the rule, including encryption, MFA, penetration testing, and a written incident response plan.

Who it applies to
CPAs, advisors, mortgage brokers, tax preparers
Enforced by
FTC (non-banks), federal banking regulators
Updated rule effective
June 9, 2023
New in 2023
Mandatory MFA, encryption, incident response plan
What the updated Safeguards Rule requires
INV
Asset inventory
Maintain a written inventory of all data systems and the customer financial information they contain or transmit.
MFA
Multi-factor authentication (required)
MFA now explicitly required for any individual accessing any information system containing customer financial information.
ENC
Encryption (required)
Customer information must be encrypted in transit and at rest. Specific standards apply to data leaving your network.
MON
Continuous monitoring or penetration testing
Either continuous monitoring of systems OR annual penetration testing plus biannual vulnerability assessments is now required.
IRP
Written incident response plan
A formal written incident response plan is now explicitly required — specifying goals, roles, and internal processes.
How Lotus addresses each GLBA requirement
Asset inventory & discovery
Security assessment produces a written inventory of every device and system — the baseline document GLBA's inventory requirement demands.
MFA configuration
We configure and verify multi-factor authentication on your accounting software, cloud accounts, email, and client portals — satisfying the explicit MFA mandate.
Continuous monitoring (satisfies testing requirement)
Our monitoring plans satisfy GLBA's continuous monitoring alternative — including vulnerability scanning oversight, patch management, and real-time alerting.
Written incident response plan
Lotus produces a written incident response plan as part of compliance readiness engagements — structured around your specific systems and the GLBA's explicit requirements.
Serving CPAs, financial advisors, tax preparers, and mortgage brokers across the Main Line.

System and Organization Controls 2
SOC 2 Type II
AICPA Trust Services Criteria — Security, Availability, Processing Integrity, Confidentiality, and Privacy

SOC 2 Type II reports are increasingly required by enterprise clients and insurers as proof that a service organization's controls are not just designed correctly but operating effectively over time. While the formal audit is conducted by a licensed CPA firm, Lotus prepares your security posture — implementing and documenting the controls that the auditor will test.

Who it applies to
Service orgs, SaaS, data processors
Audited by
Licensed CPA firms (not Lotus)
Lotus role
Pre-audit security posture & documentation
Core criterion
CC6 — Logical access security
Trust services criteria (security focus)
CC6
Logical & physical access (CC6)
The most-tested criterion. Controls over who can access what systems, how access is provisioned, and how it is revoked when someone leaves.
CC7
System operations (CC7)
Detection and monitoring of security events, vulnerability management, and incident response procedures.
CC8
Change management (CC8)
Controls ensuring that changes to systems are authorized, tested, documented, and tracked.
A1
Availability (A1)
System availability commitments and uptime — monitoring, incident response, and recovery time objectives.
How Lotus prepares you for SOC 2 audit
Access control implementation & documentation
We implement and document your access control posture — user provisioning, role-based permissions, offboarding procedures — generating the evidence trail CC6 auditors look for.
Continuous monitoring & log management
Ongoing monitoring with documented alerts and monthly reports — giving auditors evidence that CC7 controls are operating, not just written down.
Vulnerability management documentation
Patch logs, scan results, and remediation timelines — the documented change management evidence that supports CC8 and gives auditors a clear record.
Incident response plan
A written, tested incident response plan with defined roles and procedures — a required artifact for SOC 2 and one of the first documents auditors request.
